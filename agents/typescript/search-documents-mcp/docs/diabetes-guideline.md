# Type 2 Diabetes Management Guideline (MOCK — illustrative, not clinical advice)

## Glycaemic targets
For most non-pregnant adults, the general HbA1c target is below 7.0%. A less
strict target, such as below 8.0%, may be appropriate for older adults or those
with limited life expectancy, advanced complications, or a history of severe
hypoglycaemia.

## First-line therapy
Metformin, combined with lifestyle measures (diet, weight management, and
physical activity), is the preferred initial pharmacological treatment for type
2 diabetes unless contraindicated.

## Escalation
If the HbA1c target is not met after about 3 months of metformin and lifestyle
measures, add a second agent. Options include an SGLT2 inhibitor, a GLP-1
receptor agonist, a sulfonylurea, or basal insulin. The choice depends on the
patient's cardiovascular and kidney status, weight, and hypoglycaemia risk.
SGLT2 inhibitors or GLP-1 agonists are preferred when there is established heart
failure or chronic kidney disease.

## Monitoring
Check HbA1c every 3 months until it is stable and at target, then every 6
months. Review kidney function at least annually, and more often in patients
with reduced eGFR or on medications that affect the kidneys.
