# Metformin — Medication Information Sheet (MOCK — illustrative only)

## Drug class
Biguanide oral antihyperglycaemic.

## Indication
First-line pharmacological treatment for type 2 diabetes mellitus, used
alongside diet and exercise. It lowers blood glucose mainly by reducing hepatic
glucose production and improving insulin sensitivity. It does not typically cause
hypoglycaemia when used on its own.

## Dosing
Start at 500 mg once or twice daily with meals to reduce stomach upset. Increase
the dose gradually every 1 to 2 weeks as tolerated. The usual maintenance dose is
1500 to 2000 mg per day in divided doses, and the maximum recommended dose is
2550 mg per day. Extended-release formulations may be taken once daily with the
evening meal.

## Contraindications and cautions
Contraindicated when the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) is below 30
mL/min/1.73m2 because of the risk of lactic acidosis. Use with caution and do not
initiate between an eGFR of 30 and 45. Temporarily hold metformin before and for
48 hours after procedures using iodinated contrast dye, and during acute illness
involving dehydration.

## Common side effects
Gastrointestinal effects are most common: nausea, diarrhoea, abdominal
discomfort, and a metallic taste. These often improve over time or with the
extended-release form. Long-term use can lower vitamin B12 levels, so periodic
B12 monitoring is advised.

## Serious but rare
Lactic acidosis is rare but serious. Seek urgent care for unusual muscle pain,
trouble breathing, severe drowsiness, or abdominal pain with vomiting.
