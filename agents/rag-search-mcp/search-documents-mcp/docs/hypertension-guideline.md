# Hypertension Management Guideline (MOCK — illustrative, not clinical advice)

## Blood pressure categories
- Normal: systolic below 120 mmHg and diastolic below 80 mmHg.
- Elevated: systolic 120 to 129 mmHg and diastolic below 80 mmHg.
- Stage 1 hypertension: systolic 130 to 139 mmHg or diastolic 80 to 89 mmHg.
- Stage 2 hypertension: systolic 140 mmHg or higher, or diastolic 90 mmHg or
  higher.

## When to start medication
Pharmacological treatment is recommended for stage 2 hypertension, and for stage
1 hypertension when the patient has established cardiovascular disease, diabetes,
chronic kidney disease, or an estimated 10-year cardiovascular risk of 10% or
more. The general office treatment target is below 130/80 mmHg for most adults.

## First-line medications
First-line agents are thiazide or thiazide-like diuretics, ACE inhibitors,
angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs), and calcium channel blockers. ACE
inhibitors and ARBs should not be combined together. For most patients without a
compelling indication, any of these four classes is an acceptable starting
choice, and many patients eventually require two agents from different classes.

## Lifestyle modification
Recommend the DASH dietary pattern, sodium reduction to under 1500 mg per day
where feasible, regular aerobic exercise of about 150 minutes per week, weight
loss for overweight patients, and limiting alcohol intake. Lifestyle measures
alone may be trialled for 3 to 6 months in elevated or low-risk stage 1
patients before adding medication.

## Monitoring
Recheck blood pressure within 2 to 4 weeks of starting or changing therapy.
Check electrolytes and kidney function within 2 to 4 weeks of starting an ACE
inhibitor, ARB, or diuretic.
